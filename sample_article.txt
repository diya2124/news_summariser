Karnataka Launches Free Digital Library Scheme for Rural Students (fictional test article)

Bengaluru: The Karnataka government on Monday announced a new scheme to provide free access to digital libraries for students in rural areas, aiming to reduce the learning gap between villages and cities.

Under the scheme, 2,500 gram panchayat offices across the state will be equipped with computers, high-speed internet and e-book collections in Kannada, English and Hindi. Students from classes 6 to 12 will be able to use the facilities free of cost between 9 am and 6 pm on weekdays.

The Education Minister said the programme will cost around Rs 320 crore over three years and will be rolled out in phases. In the first phase, 500 panchayats in north Karnataka districts will get the facilities by March next year. Each centre will also have a trained local volunteer to help students use the resources.

Education experts welcomed the move but said regular maintenance and reliable electricity would decide whether the scheme succeeds. "Many earlier projects failed because computers were installed but not maintained," said a retired school headmaster in Dharwad.

The government said teachers will also be trained to include digital resources in their lessons. A mobile app that lets students borrow e-books and track their reading will be launched in six months.
